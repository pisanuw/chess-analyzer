# Improvement report (round 2): frontend, backend, opponent-preparation pedagogy

A fresh, read-only review of the whole codebase at commit `8da94c3` (2026-09-12): every server module, every view, the shared helpers, the service worker, the tests, and the publish scripts. The previous report was verified item by item and retired; nothing it covered is repeated here, and the four cleanups already parked in BRIEFING next step 7 (the play-out refactor, manual LLM mode, ECO-based `phaseOf`, the legacy password) are not repeated either. Nothing here proposes a build step, a framework, an API-key provider, or a second frontend-backend boundary. Items are ranked within each section, most valuable first; the ones marked **bug** were confirmed against the code, not inferred. File and line references are as of that commit and will drift.

## Frontend

1. **The Prepare page is not usable offline, which is where it is needed.** `public/sw.js` caches only `/api/drills`, `/api/auth/me`, `/api/status`, and `/api/settings`; the Prepare page fetches `/api/prep/<subject>?color=...` and `/api/upcoming`, and the prep sheet, the predicted lines, and the deck are exactly what a player wants in a hall with no signal. `api.prepMark` failures are swallowed (`prep.js:180`, `:199`), so offline deck work is lost too. Add prefix matches for `/api/prep/`, `/api/upcoming`, and `/api/scout/` to `OFFLINE_API` (network first, so nothing changes online), and queue prep marks the way `enqueueReview` queues grades (same localStorage queue, a `kind` field, replayed by `flushReviews`).

2. **The deck has no order of importance and no second pass that focuses on misses.** `prep.js` serves `[...deck.lines, ...deck.punish]` in the server's ply order (`buildLineDrills` sorts by ply, `prep.js:79`), and "Go through it again" (`prep.js:150`) restarts at card 1 including cards already answered right. Sort line cards by the line's likelihood (see Pedagogy 4) and, on a repeat pass, serve cards with `right === 0` first, then the rest: a Leitner pass inside the deck, using the marks the page already holds.

3. **The theme control is unreachable on a bare local run.** The Auto/Light/Dark buttons live inside the account menu, and `renderWhoami` hides that menu whenever auth is off (`app.js:138`), which is every single-user local install. Move the theme row to the Settings page (or always show a minimal menu with just the theme when there is no user).

4. **Chart tooltips are mouse-only.** `charts.js` binds `mousemove` for the bar chart (`:74`), the line chart (`:124`), and the eval graph (`:198`); on a phone the accuracy trend, the rating trend, and the error-type bars show no detail at all, and the `sub` text (the game behind a point) is unreachable. Use pointer events and a tap-to-pin tooltip (second tap or tap outside clears), which also fixes the eval graph's hover on tablets.

5. **The home-screen icon does not work on iOS.** `index.html:10` points `apple-touch-icon` at `/icon.svg`; iOS ignores SVG there, so an installed PWA (the tournament-hall phone) gets a screenshot tile. Ship a 180x180 PNG for Apple and 192/512 PNGs in `manifest.webmanifest` (the SVG can stay as the `any` entry).

6. **Polling never pauses.** `pollJobs` fires every 5 s idle and 1.5 s active and `updateDrillBadge` every 60 s (`app.js:120`, `:247`) regardless of `document.visibilityState`, on every open tab, for the life of the session. Pause both on `visibilitychange` hidden and resume (with an immediate poll) on visible; the same hook should also stop `pollClash`.

7. **The clash renderer is a view imported by another view.** `renderClashForest`, the tree format, the lichess format, `clashVarMoves`, and the board wiring (about 300 of `scout.js`'s 828 lines) are imported by `views/prep.js` from `views/scout.js`, the coupling `labels.js` was created to avoid. Extract them to `public/clashview.js` (the same shape as `playout.js`): `scout.js` becomes the Players page, the clash rendering gets its own smoke-test step, and the Prepare page stops depending on the Players page module.

8. **Full page reloads where a re-render would do.** Adding or removing an upcoming game (`prep.js:105`, `:109`) and linking a FIDE id (`scout.js:352`) call `location.reload()`, which loses scroll position and every open accordion. Re-run the view (`dispatchEvent(new HashChangeEvent('hashchange'))`, as `game.js:153` does after a name fix).

9. **The smoke test never exercises an engine-backed flow, although a fake engine exists.** `test/fixtures/fake-stockfish.mjs` drives `test/engine.test.js`, but `test/ui/smoke.mjs` starts the server without `STOCKFISH_PATH`, so Play it out, the play-out drills (conversion/defence/endgame), the off-list quick eval in a guess, and the clash "extend" button are all untested at the UI level. Point the smoke server at the fixture and add four steps; also open the clash card once (its tree render is the most intricate DOM on the site and has no browser coverage).

10. **Home shows only the first upcoming game.** A tournament weekend is two or three rounds; `home.js:31` picks one entry and one prep. List every dated game within the next seven days, each with its deck progress and sheet status, and show days-to-go so the plan in Pedagogy 7 has somewhere to live.

11. **Small accessibility gaps in the Players table.** The prep-readiness dot is colour plus a `title` (`scout.js:61`), which touch and screen-reader users do not get; add visually hidden text ("ready", "to do"). The colour-cut and time-control buttons (`scout.js:167`, `prep.js:51`) are toggles without `aria-pressed`.

## Backend

1. **Bug: drill re-sync after a settings change only touches the default member.** `PUT /api/settings` re-scores every member's games (`resummarizeGames` walks `listAllGames()`) but then calls `syncAllDrills()` with no user (`routes/games.js:116`), so only `kai`'s ladder is rebuilt; Nikash's and Neeraj's drills keep the old moments and tiers until the next server restart (where `serve.js` loops `listMembers()`). Loop the members here too.

2. **Bug: fixing a game's colour or names syncs its drills into the wrong store.** `POST /api/games/:id/player` (`routes/games.js:214`) and `POST /api/games/:id/names` (`:238`) call `syncDrillsForGame(game, settings)` without the owner, so a colour fix on Nikash's game writes drills into `data/users/kai/drills.json` and never updates Nikash's. The analyse job gets this right (`jobs.js:164` passes `saved.owner`). Pass `game.owner || DEFAULT_USER` in both routes and add a `multiuser.test.js` case for each.

3. **Bug: PGN dates are still string-sorted in four places.** `scoutbook.js` fixed the unpadded-date problem ("2026.7.29" sorts after "2026.10.01") with `pgnToDate`, and the Games page has its own `dnum`, but the server still uses `localeCompare` in `store.js:213` (the games list order), `report.js:74` (the chronological order behind `timeline`, the accuracy trend, and the "recent versus earlier" `categoryTrend`, which therefore compares the wrong halves for anyone whose PGNs are unpadded), `repertoire.js:55`, and `subjects.js:96` (head-to-head order). Put one `pgnDateKey(dateStr)` in `public/shared.js`, use it in all four and in `games.js`, and delete the frontend copy.

4. **Model calls run inside HTTP requests and a browser tab drives the bulk work.** Prep-sheet generation, clash narration, re-explain, and `pattern-notes/sync` each hold a request open for the whole model call (minutes, no Express timeout), and the Admin card's "Extend & narrate all" is a `for` loop in `admin.js:129`: close the tab and the run stops, and none of it appears in the job list with a cost line or a cancel. The job queue already has lanes; add an `llm` lane with kinds `prepsheet`, `narrate`, and `patterns` (the same `enqueue` dedup on a synthetic id), have those routes enqueue and return `{ job }`, and let the header's poller show progress and `costUsd` as it does for explain jobs. The Admin card then queues everything server-side in one click.

5. **Startup resume spends the subscription without being asked.** `resumeInterrupted` (`jobs.js:91`) enqueues an explain job for every game in status `analysed` when `autoExplain` is on. After a restart with a backlog (CHANGES records a 74-game one) that is 74 model calls nobody chose, which contradicts the Admin card's "the endpoint only reports, so nothing starts by accident". Resume only work that was actually interrupted: games in `analysing`, and explains that were running at shutdown (stamp `explainStartedAt` on the game when `runExplain` begins and clear it at the end). Leave the backlog to the Admin card.

6. **`predictionFor` rebuilds the student index and forest per game.** `headToHead` calls it once per own game against the subject (`subjects.js:93`), every dossier and Prepare view calls `headToHead`, and `GET /api/games/:id` calls it again; each call loads every student game (cache-backed) and assembles a forest. Memoise the "others" forest on `(userId, fideId, indexFingerprint of the student's games minus this one, bookImportedAt)` through `memo.js`; only the `walkPrediction` walk is per game.

7. **Prep marks rewrite the whole hosted drill row per card.** `markPrep` (`drills.js:445`) reads and CAS-writes the entire `drills:<id>` row (hundreds of drills, guesses, feedback) for each deck answer; on the mirror that is a full Supabase round trip from a phone per card, and a busy deck can lose CAS races. Keep prep marks in their own key (`prep:<id>`, the pattern `upcoming.js` uses) or batch them client-side (flush every few cards and on `pagehide`).

8. **Route hygiene now that the local server is multi-user.** `GET /api/jobs` (`routes/games.js:437`) is unwrapped and ungated, so a visitor or any member sees every other member's game ids, stages, and error text; `GET /api/status` returns `dataDir` and the engine path to visitors; `POST /api/playout/move` and `/assess` (Stockfish CPU) are open to visitors with no throttle. Filter the job list to games the caller can see (or admin-only for the raw list), drop `dataDir` for non-admins, and put the playout routes behind `rateLimit(req, 'playout')` or `blockVisitor`. While there: magic-link tokens are replayable for 15 minutes (documented in `magiclink.js`); a one-time `used:<tokenHash>` marker in the KV store (in-memory locally) closes it in ten lines.

9. **One sparring engine serves every interactive verdict.** `getSparringEngine` is a single 1-thread process with a serial command queue (`engine.js:176`), shared by play-outs (700 ms per move), off-list quick evals (3 s), and drill verdicts (3 s). With three members on the local server, one member's play-out queues another member's drill answer behind it. A pool of two (or a dedicated process for quick evals, which are the ones a human is staring at) removes the stall without touching the analysis pool.

10. **Stale comment and a subset mismatch in the clash index.** `clashindex.js:27` still says "No Elo-band filter here", but `buildOpponentIndex` applies `withinElo` (`:89`, `:100`). And `featureCollector` defines the opponent's main lines from the recency window alone (`inWindow`, `:141`) while `parsed()` only ever sees the Elo-banded games, so `inBook`/`outOfBook` scores are measured against main lines defined on a different set. Define `mainLines` on the same filter and fix the comment.

11. **Test gaps worth closing.** No test covers the two multi-user sync paths in items 1 and 2 (the analyse-job path is covered, the route paths are not); `repeatedDeviations` and the head-to-head `prediction` summary (`subjects.js:57`, `:107`) have no direct test; `public/sw.js` has none (a 30-line test with a fake `caches` and `fetch` would pin the network-first and navigate-fallback rules); and the date-key helper from item 3 needs the unpadded case. `prep.test.js` should gain the deviation case from Pedagogy 1.

## Pedagogical: preparing for a specific opponent

1. **Bug: a line flashcard can rehearse the student's own mistake.** `buildLineDrills` (`server/prep.js:32`) takes the student's own continuation at each node as the answer key (`candidates = node.edges...`, `:45`) and ignores `edge.deviation`, the flag `buildStudentIndex` sets when that move left the engine's lines or lost 10 or more win-probability points in the opening (`clashindex.js:61`). In a line where the student went wrong at move 7 in two games, the card asks "what do you play here?" and marks the losing move correct, with a "your line" chip. Fix: at a student node whose kept edge is flagged, use the engine lines when the leaf has been extended (`node.engineLines`, accepted set within `WP_ACCEPT`) and label the card "repair: in your games you played X here and lost ground"; when no engine lines exist, skip the card and list the node under "lines to repair" with the Extend button. Add the case to `prep.test.js`.

2. **A sparring partner that plays their book, not just their rating.** Play-outs call Stockfish at `UCI_Elo` (`routes/games.js:406`), so the student rehearses against "someone rated 2050". The opponent index the Prepare page already loads (`oppIndex[oppColor][posKey]`) knows, for every position they have reached, what they play and how often. Add `mode: 'book'` to `POST /api/playout/move` (with `fideId`): while the position is in their index, reply with a move sampled by weighted share (or the top reply, a toggle), and fall back to the Elo-limited engine only when out of book. Early deviations then appear at their real frequency, the move list can say "book move (34%, 12 games)" versus "engine", and the play-out finally trains what "Guess their move" trains: predicting the person. Engine- and data-grounded, no model call.

3. **Prepare for an opponent with no data, the actual "new opponent" case.** `GET /api/prep/:subject` returns 404 unless there is a scout report, a book, or a head-to-head record (`routes/prep.js:33`), so a stranger from the pairing list gets nothing. Offer a rating-band prep from the student's own data: their games against opponents within about 100 points of the entered rating in that colour (which openings scored well, where their own prep ended, their phase weaknesses against that band), the S-only version of matchup risks, and a deck of their own opening flashcards (`kind: 'opening'`, already built by `syncDrillsForGame`) plus sharpen drills in the colour. `addUpcoming` needs a `rating` field for this. The fastest route to a real book for a stranger is BRIEFING step 4 (lichess and chess.com username import): `buildScoutBook` needs only parsed games and a key, so widening the store key from FIDE-id-only (`isFideId` in `store.js:82`) to also accept `lichess:<user>` and `chesscom:<user>` is the enabling change.

4. **Allocate preparation time by probability.** `clashPrincipalLines` computes a `likelihood` per line (the product of the opponent's shares along it, `clash.js:195`), but the deck sorts line cards by ply and drops the number, and the prep sheet's C facts do not carry it. Show "reached in about 30% of their games" on every line card, sort the deck by likelihood (weighted down once a card is marked right), and put the likelihood in the C evidence text so the sheet's openings table can lead with the most probable line. Add one tile per colour, "how predictable they are": the share-weighted entropy of their replies at their first three decision nodes (low means learn lines, high means learn plans), next to the historical hit rate `headToHead.prediction` already computes.

5. **An endgame profile of the opponent, from the book, without an engine.** The clash job parses every recent book game; `materialSignature` (`report.js:364`) already buckets the student's own endgame trouble. In `featureCollector.parsed`, take the signature at the first ply `phaseOf` calls endgame and tally signature, games, and score. That yields F facts such as "reaches rook endings in 31% of games and scores 38% there (21 games)", a habits tile, and, crossed with the student's own `report.endgames`, a concrete structure for `matchup_risks` to steer toward or away from.

6. **How they lose, and how long they resist.** From analysed games (`curveFor` in `tendencies.js`): the median number of plies they played on after their win probability first fell under 10 (grinder versus early resigner), and the share of their losses that were collapses (a 40-point swing inside 10 plies, which `swings()` already detects) versus slow slides. From the book: the average length of their losses versus their wins. As T facts these let the must-win framing say "complicate: five of their eight losses were collapses inside ten plies" instead of quoting the overall conversion rate.

7. **Spread the deck over the days before the game.** `upcoming.js` stores the date and the deck has seen/right marks, but nothing connects them. Derive a plan from days-to-go: five or more days out, the whole deck; two days out, misses and the three most likely lines; the morning of the game, the sheet quiz (next item) and the top lines, ten minutes. Show it on Home ("Saturday vs X: 4 cards to revisit today") next to the games from Frontend 10, and make "Go through it again" mean misses first (Frontend 2).

8. **Active recall of the sheet itself.** The sheet is read, never tested, and its fields are structured (`profile` rows, `openings` rows with when/play/why, `watch_fors`), so a four-question quiz needs no model: "Their weakest phase?" (profile), "Against their <when>, you play?" (reveal `play`, self-graded), "Name two cues to watch for" (reveal, self-graded), "Which of these lines do they play most?" (from the book shares). Record it as a prep mark `sheet:<subject>` so the day-of plan can require it. Testing beats re-reading for retention, and this is the one artefact the player will try to recall at the board.

9. **A post-game prep debrief that feeds the next sheet.** "Did the prep hold?" reports where the game left the tree. Extend `predictionFor` with a deterministic deck check: match the game's positions (`posKeyOf(fenBefore)`) against the deck's line-card FENs, punish-drill FENs, and sparring positions, and report "4 of 12 deck positions occurred; you played the card answer in 3; the critical moment at move 23 was outside every prepared line". Store it on the game as `prepDebrief`, show it in the head-to-head, and cite it as an H fact so a rematch sheet can say "last time the prepared positions you missed were ...".

10. **Cut the analysed subset by time control too.** `buildPrep` passes `tc` only to `scoutDossier` (`prep.js:146`); `gamesForSubject`, `buildReport`, `buildRepertoire`, and `dueDrills` ignore it, so a rapid-heavy scout subset feeds tendencies, punish drills, and the sheet's E, P, and T facts into a classical prep. Scout game files carry `headers.TimeControl`; classify with `classifyTimeControl` and filter in those four when `tc` is set (the memo keys must include it).

11. **Rehearse their clock, not just their moves.** The pacing curve (`clockByMove`) says when they typically get short; drills have a fixed 30-second timed mode (`TIMED_SECONDS`) but play-outs have no clock at all. Give the book-driven sparring mode (item 2) an optional clock: the student gets a budget per move derived from their own pacing, and the engine "thinks" for a time drawn from the opponent's curve at that move number, so "they are usually under ten minutes by move 30" becomes a situation the student has actually sat in.

## Suggested next steps

Fix the four confirmed bugs first, all small: the deviation flashcard (Pedagogy 1), the two drill-sync owner bugs (Backend 1 and 2), and the date key (Backend 3), each with a test. Then the two offline pieces that make the Prepare page a tournament tool (Frontend 1 and 5), and the theme control (Frontend 3), an afternoon between them. The larger pedagogy items build on each other in this order: likelihood on the deck (Pedagogy 4) and the book-driven sparring partner (Pedagogy 2) reuse data already in memory; the day-of plan and the sheet quiz (Pedagogy 7 and 8) then have something to schedule; the prep debrief (Pedagogy 9) closes the loop. The `llm` job lane (Backend 4) and the resume fix (Backend 5) are the two backend changes that most affect the operator's day and subscription.
